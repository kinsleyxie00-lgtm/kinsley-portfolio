# KINSLEY XIE Portfolio — Aquatic Botanical Archive Hero

## Objective

Refine the approved Living Tree Hero into a quieter, more editorial botanical archive. Replace the current dark conservatory-film aesthetic with a visual system combining botanical scientific plates, aqueous reflection, aged natural material, and restrained fragrance-editorial photography.

Generate and review the new base image before it is installed in the website. This phase does not alter the existing page structure or later portfolio chapters.

## Reference Interpretation

The three supplied images are visual-language references rather than compositions to copy.

Extract:

- warm bone-white paper and low-contrast natural light;
- specimen-like plants intersecting a clean waterline;
- botanical annotation leaders and delicate editorial typography;
- wet surfaces, aqueous refraction, old wood, dark green liquid, and fibrous grass;
- imperfect printed-photograph texture and restrained olive, moss, charcoal, and cream tones;
- close material observation instead of a scenic forest view.

Avoid literal cups, fruit, triptych panels, copied labels, or recreation of any reference photograph.

## Selected Direction

Use a hybrid of botanical scientific plate and dark sensory material archive.

The ancient tree remains the interface and spatial anchor, but the surrounding environment becomes an ambiguous museum-laboratory installation rather than a glass conservatory. A controlled water plane, archival background, and small botanical annotations create the premium editorial character.

## Master Image Composition

Create one wide photographic master suitable for a desktop Hero.

- Camera stands approximately 1–1.5 metres from a monumental, partial old tree root or lower trunk.
- The wood exceeds the frame; no complete tree or landscape is visible.
- A precise horizontal waterline crosses part of the composition. Some root material enters the water and produces physically plausible refraction and a subdued reflection.
- The primary bark remains tactile, irregular, aged, slightly damp, and architecturally scaled.
- Four distinct clusters of small compound leaves emerge directly from believable bark fissures and root seams.
- Leave controlled negative space for the existing KINSLEY XIE identity.
- The background suggests bone-white botanical plate paper, mineral plaster, or a museum display surface rather than a room or forest.

The scene must read as one photographed installation, not a collage, mood board, triptych, or ecommerce banner.

## Four Navigation Growth Points

The generated photograph must contain four separable leaf clusters in irregular, botanically plausible positions:

1. ABOUT near a lower root seam.
2. EXPERIENCE following an upward trunk fissure.
3. CASE on a lateral root or branch structure.
4. NOTES at the highest visible new growth point.

Each cluster must be large and isolated enough to support a local grayscale overlay and a minimum 44px interactive target. The photograph supplies the real leaves; no illustrated or CSS-drawn bud icons are permitted.

Initial website state uses a local waxy grey-white treatment over each real leaf cluster. Activation fades that treatment to reveal the same photographed leaves in low-saturation olive green. Explored clusters remain green for the current page session.

## Editorial Annotation System

Chapter labels behave like botanical-plate annotations:

- hairline leaders extend from the actual growth point;
- small uppercase sans-serif chapter indexes remain secondary;
- optional italic serif botanical microcopy appears only on hover or focus;
- labels avoid UI badges, circles, icon containers, and regular grid alignment.

The labels must feel printed and specimen-oriented while remaining legible and keyboard accessible.

## Depth and Motion

The single master image will be separated into restrained visual depth layers:

- background paper or mineral plane moves slightly opposite the pointer;
- primary trunk is nearly stable;
- water reflection shifts independently with minimal horizontal distortion;
- foreground bark and leaf edges move farther than the trunk;
- local interactive leaf overlays remain registered to their corresponding bark positions.

Pointer response must be visible but slow. It should create spatial parallax, not a tilted card effect. No particles, glow, fantasy lighting, or game-like camera motion.

On selection:

1. The grey-white local leaf treatment recedes to olive green.
2. The real leaf cluster breathes and opens subtly.
3. The camera approaches along the botanical annotation line.
4. Water refraction expands or a leaf surface crosses the frame.
5. The existing matching portfolio chapter is revealed.

## Palette and Finish

- bone white and aged cream;
- charcoal and near-black green;
- moss, lichen, and muted olive;
- weathered brown-grey wood;
- glassy water transparency.

Keep saturation low and highlights soft. Prefer printed editorial grain, delicate tonal separation, and natural surface irregularity over cinematic contrast.

## Image Generation Constraints

The generation prompt must explicitly avoid:

- complete trees, forest scenery, wallpaper, or landscape photography;
- glass conservatories as a dominant setting;
- fantasy, magical glow, particles, or cinematic-game imagery;
- decorative floral arrangements and skincare products;
- visible coffee, fruit, branded objects, or literal reference-image collage;
- 2D illustrated buds, vector icons, or interface graphics;
- text, logos, watermarks, and illegible generated annotations;
- saturated emerald green or glossy commercial advertising treatment.

## Review Gate

Generate the new image as a preview-only asset first. Inspect it for scale, material credibility, negative space, waterline realism, four usable leaf clusters, and absence of collage or fantasy styling. Show the image to the user before copying it into the project or modifying the Hero implementation.

## Later Validation

After a generated image is separately approved and implemented, validate desktop and mobile crops, pointer parallax, four growth targets, grey-white-to-green activation, camera approach, organic chapter transition, visited-state retention, reduced motion, navigation restoration, TypeScript, and production build. Keep all work local and do not deploy.
